About

I am an Internal Medicine resident at Case Western/University Hospitals pursuing a fellowship in Hematology/Oncology. My interests sit at the intersection of metabolic health in cancer, applied AI for population oncology, and early-onset GI cancers.

Primary Research Interests

  1. Early-onset GI cancers and the role of metabolic health in malignancy
  2. Applied AI in Oncology: LLMs for Population Oncology (data abstraction to collect, aggregate, and analyze cancer data at scale), and AI for predicting and preventing cancer

Other Interests

Clinical trial improvement, preventive oncology, access to cancer care in rural areas and low- and middle-income countries, AI/LLM clinical decision-making, precision medicine, and transparency and competitiveness in healthcare.

One focus of my research is how metabolic dysfunction, including obesity, insulin resistance, and fatty liver disease, drives early-onset colorectal cancer. My current work examines how these metabolic drivers alter tumor biology, raise cancer risk, and influence metastatic progression. I believe metabolic dysfunction creates a state of accelerated tumorigenesis through systemic metabolic remodeling, persistent low-level inflammation, and shifts in the gut microbiome. Targeted metabolic therapies, like GLP-1 receptor agonists, may mitigate this risk. I have led projects on distress screening gaps in EO-CRC at our institution and on chemoprevention strategies in CRC. I work with wonderful mentors including Dr. Melissa Lumish and Dr. Gregory Cooper at Case Western Reserve/University Hospitals.

I believe applied AI and large-scale data can advance cancer prevention and early detection by identifying risk signals and biomarkers that reveal chemoprevention opportunities. At my institution, I am building a secure large language model (LLM) pipeline that turns unstructured clinical records into research-grade data by extracting clinicogenomic variables with frontier and open-weight models.

Open to collaboration on applied AI for cancer care, early-onset GI cancers, and metabolic oncology.
